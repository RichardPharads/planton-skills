# Step-by-step plans (`"type": "steps"`)

For anything done once, in a fixed order: recipes and baking, installing or setting something up, repairs,
applications and paperwork, a first-time task. Write it so exactly that a first-timer can follow it on their phone
without guessing.

## Questions

Up to 3, skipping anything the request already says:

- **Scale**: servings, size, quantity, or number of rooms or devices.
- **Equipment**: what they have (oven or air fryer, drill or screwdriver, phone or computer).
- **Experience**: first time (explain every technique) or done before (shorter notes).

## Get the facts right first

Quantities that scale correctly, standard times and temperatures, the official requirements for paperwork. When a
detail varies by place or product (a form's current version, a device model, local rules), tell the user to check it
and add a "Check: …" bullet. Don't guess.

## Write the steps

- **4–15 steps.** The first gathers what's needed, titled like "Gather the ingredients" or "Get your documents ready",
  with a checklist of every item and its exact amount or specification. The last finishes the job: serve, test, verify
  it works, clean up, and how to store or maintain.
- **One action per step.** If a step has "and then", split it.
- **Each step's notes, in this order:**
  1. A paragraph with exactly how: amounts, sizes, heat level, settings, where to click, how long.
  2. A bullet **"Done when: …"** describing what the person will see, smell, hear, or measure.
  3. Optional bullets **"Watch for: …"** (the common mistake or warning sign) and **"If …: …"** (what to do when
     something goes differently). Don't create branches; describe alternatives in bullets.
  4. Checklist items the person ticks as they go, one per action with its own amount (usually 2–10), each
     observable. See "Be specific" in [writing.md](../../planton/reference/writing.md).
- **Timers**: `timerMinutes` on every step that's mostly waiting (marinate, rise, simmer, cure, dry, charge). For waits
  longer than 24 hours, say so in the notes instead.
- **Safety as a checklist item** where it matters: safe internal temperature for meat and poultry (e.g. chicken 74 °C /
  165 °F), turning off power at the breaker, gloves and ventilation for chemicals.
- Use cards inside a step only when a step has several parallel parts ("Prepare the sides" with three sides).
- Put the total time and difficulty in the workflow's `description` ("About 1½ hours · Beginner").

## Review before saving

Walk through the steps once as a beginner would. Check that every ingredient or item used in a step appears in the
first step, amounts add up, and timers match the text.
